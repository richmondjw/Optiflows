What to say

Three message layers, your opener and how to use proof.

The message, in three layers

1. Lead with the mission

Keep critical operations connected — across vehicles, field teams and remote assets.

2. Make the stand line tangible

Hybrid connectivity. Built for uptime. It is on the stand wall, so visitors will read it before they speak to you. Explain it through their reality: coverage that changes, remote sites, vehicle fleets, more than one network, and applications that must keep working.

3. Prove it carefully

We are supporting real rollouts and evaluations across emergency services, utilities, mining and remote operations. Name the stage every time — rollout, PoC, tender or assessment. Never flatten them into "wins".

Your opener

We help emergency services and other critical operators keep vehicles, field teams and remote assets connected when one network or one coverage path is not enough. We combine resilient cellular, satellite and edge networking with deployment support through trusted channel partners. What part of your operation becomes most exposed when connectivity drops?

Three conversation plays

Emergency services and command vehicles

Ask about fleet coverage, dispatch, video, telemetry and continuity during an incident. Internal proof themes: ambulance and SES rollouts; fire, police and CFS evaluations.

Utilities and field operations

Ask where crews and vehicles lose coverage and what must keep working in a remote location. Internal proof theme: SA Water's VaaN rollout.

Forestry, mining and remote assets

Ask how machinery and mobile assets are monitored and what an outage costs. Ask whether they are exploring VaaN. Internal proof themes: Qube Forestry and Santos.

Discovery questions

  • Which vehicles, sites or teams cannot tolerate a loss of connectivity?
  • Where do you see weak coverage, congestion or carrier dependence?
  • What must continue during an outage: voice, video, telemetry, dispatch, applications — or all of them?
  • Are you looking at multi-carrier resilience, satellite, cellular failover or a full VaaN architecture?
  • What stage are you at: discovery, PoC, budget, tender or rollout?
  • Who else needs to be involved?

Say this, not that

Say Avoid
"We are supporting rollouts and evaluations in this space." Calling a PoC, tender or assessment a win or a customer

"Let's agree a next step before you leave." "I'll send you some information" with nothing noted

Qualify and close

Spot the priority conversation, handle pushback and leave with a next step.

Six stand rules

  1. Conversation before collateral. Start with a relevant question, not a brochure.
  2. Help first, qualify second. Make the visitor's problem clearer before you collect details.
  3. Evidence before enthusiasm. Use the right stage label for every example.
  4. Every conversation is captured. Log it in the scanning app or take comprehensive notes, so we can consolidate afterwards.
  5. Bring in the right specialist early. Hand off for product, carrier, partner or technical depth.
  6. No unsupported claims. No improvised pricing, endorsements, competitor comparisons, ROI or capability.

Is this conversation a priority?

Look for several of these signals:

  • A material continuity or coverage problem
  • A defined vehicle, site, asset or fleet
  • Meaningful scale or operational consequence
  • A project stage — discovery, PoC, budget, tender or rollout
  • Named stakeholders and a credible timeframe
  • Agreement to a specific next action
After the event: lead triage

In the days straight after the event, we download the leads from the lead scanning app. Peter and Eerin triage them across the group, and the leads go into Zoho CRM almost immediately.

Your job on the stand: make sure every lead is submitted in the app or covered by comprehensive notes, so nothing is lost before triage.

What to capture before you walk away

Organisation · operating environment · fleet or asset count · the problem · networks and applications in play · project stage · stakeholders · partner involved · what was agreed · your name.

When they push back

"We already have a carrier."

Ask where coverage, resilience or application continuity is still exposed, then explore multi-network or hybrid options.

"We are only researching."

Offer a useful discovery conversation. Find out the operational trigger, the scale and the decision timing — without forcing a sales stage.

"Send me information."

Ask which operational problem the information should address. Agree what to send and note it in the lead record.

"We are comparing providers."

Acknowledge it. Return to requirements, operating environment, resilience model and deployment support. Do not attack competitors.

After the conversation

04

Lead scanning app

Install it before you travel. Press SUBMIT after every scan.

Rule of the app

Press SUBMIT after every scan. If you don't, the lead is not saved.

Set it up before you travel

Comms Connect gives each exhibitor user their own lead-scanning login. Do this at home, on the phone you will carry on the stand.

  • 1. Download the app. Search The Event App by EventsAIR in the Apple App Store or Google Play.
  • 2. Enter the event code when the app first opens. Your sales manager will give you the code.
  • 3. Allow camera access when prompted. Scanning needs it.
  • 4. Log in with your own credentials. Each person uses their own login name and PIN. Do not share or borrow a colleague's.
  • 5. Open Scan Leads and test it on the practice QR code in the organiser's setup email.
Sales manager to complete

Individual logins. Each attending rep needs their own login and PIN from the organiser, and the event code needs to reach them by a private channel. Codes and PINs are deliberately not published on this page. Request any missing logins before travel: Julie, juliew@melbevents.com.au.

Scanning a visitor

  1. Open Scan Leads.
  2. Hold the camera over the QR code on the visitor's badge.
  3. Answer any pre-programmed questions that appear.
  4. Add a note: their problem, stage and the agreed next step.
  5. Press SUBMIT.

What the scan does and does not do

  • It captures the visitor's contact details, your answers and your notes.
  • You see basic details of the people you scanned. Only the company's main exhibition contact can see the full lead database.
  • A scan is a starting point, not a lead. Your note is what makes it usable: organisation, problem, stage and what was agreed. Everything is consolidated after the event.
Sales leadership to complete

Stand contact and questions. Confirm who our main exhibition contact is, and what pre-programmed qualifying questions they set (suggest: stage, fleet or asset size, project timing, partner involved). Also confirm how scanned leads reach Zoho and who owns follow-up.

If something goes wrong

  • Before the event: email Julie at the address above.
  • During the event: go to the registration desk.
  • Screenshot or jot down any lead you cannot save, and hand it to the event lead the same day.
