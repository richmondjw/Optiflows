# Comms Connect sales briefing: section grader

Version 1.0, 30 September 2026. Scope: the five sales-communication sections (00–04) of the sales-leadership review draft. The goal is a useful stand briefing, with the event timetable kept as a reference. The grader reads the page for context and scores each of sections 00–04 independently. James explicitly excluded unfinished timetable and availability work from this pass, so section 05 is preserved and not scored.

## Scores

Each dimension is scored from 0 to 100. A section passes only when **every dimension is at least 95**, not merely when the average is 95. Scores are evidence-based: cite a short phrase or location for each deduction. A 95 means ready for sales-leadership review; 100 means no meaningful editorial defect found. This is an editorial quality threshold, not an assertion that event facts or claims have been independently approved. An external input still pending (a shift schedule, approved product list, or standard follow-up deadline) is not itself a writing defect when the draft states the owner, the interim safe instruction, and the pre-distribution gate. Score the clarity of that treatment, not completion of the external decision.

| Dimension | What 95+ requires | Typical deductions |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | The section has one clear job, follows naturally from the prior section, and points to the next action or reference without a gap or contradiction. | An unexplained jump, a broken section reference, a rule changed elsewhere, or a workflow with a missing handoff. |
| Clarity | A rep can understand the words on first reading; headings and actions are specific; acronyms and placeholders are explained or clearly assigned. | Ambiguous owner, vague instruction, undefined jargon, editorial notes presented as final rep copy. |
| Tightness and organisation | One home for each substantive message; useful overview repetition only; content can be scanned on a phone; detail sits in the appropriate section. | Verbatim repeated opener, repeated capture instructions, bloated lists, timetable mixed into the pitch. |
| Actionability | The section helps a rep make a better decision or perform a concrete step at the stand. | Generic motivation without action, a checklist lacking an owner/next step, missing failure path for an operational procedure. |
| Evidence and confidentiality | Claims stay within supplied evidence and Eerin/Kat's boundary. Named customers or projects require prior approval for this audience; Peplink partner project details stay off the stand; unapproved counts and outcomes are absent. | Unsupported deployment claim, named project, confidential partner link, or advice inviting a rep to use internal examples. |

## Critical gates

A named customer/project from internal updates, an unapproved count/outcome, a contradiction of the confidentiality rule, or broken lead-save instruction caps Evidence and confidentiality at 80 and fails the section. Missing live roster, event times, or the submit instruction fails the deterministic preservation check. A grader cannot award a pass when a critical gate fails, even if prose is otherwise strong.

## Loop

1. Extract current visible section text from the built page and run deterministic preservation/confidentiality checks.
2. Run a **fresh read-only Codex grading invocation** using this frozen rubric and all six sections as context. The author does not assign scores.
3. Record per-section, per-dimension scores and specific deductions in a versioned receipt.
4. Revise only the failing section and any linked section needed to remove a contradiction. Repeat steps 1–3 with a new invocation.
5. Stop at the first full pass. Publish only after checking the live page separately; a grader pass does not prove the deployment or factual approval.

If the model grader is unavailable, record that limitation. Do not manufacture a score or treat deterministic checks as a substitute for editorial grading.
