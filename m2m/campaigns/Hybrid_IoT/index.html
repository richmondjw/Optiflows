The Opportunity

Why this campaign exists now, who we built it for, and how the target list was assembled.

Why now

Iridium has just released the 9604, the first module to combine Cat-M1 / LTE-M cellular, Iridium SBD satellite, and GNSS in one chip. For the first time, an IoT device can run on cellular wherever cellular works and fall back to satellite when it doesn't — without a dual-stack design and without separate airtime contracts.

That is a moment. M2M can sell more than the module. We can support the full hybrid stack — module access + cellular airtime + satellite airtime + hardware advice + integration support — under a single commercial relationship. That is a stronger story than anyone else in the channel is telling right now.

The play

Install base first. Not a broad market push. Start with customers who already have at least one signal that hybrid IoT might solve a real problem for them:

  • Cat-M1 / NB-IoT / low-data SIM users
  • Iridium SBD / Edge / satellite hardware customers
  • Remote, regional, mobile or roaming deployments
  • Fleet, asset tracking, agtech, environmental monitoring, utilities, field safety
  • Device OEMs, SIs, VARs and platform companies
  • Anyone who has asked about coverage gaps, fallback, blackspots or satellite cost

The objective is to book Hybrid IoT Readiness Reviews with high-fit customers — a 30-minute conversation to assess whether Cat-M1 + Iridium SBD makes sense for their device, deployment or roadmap.

Use Cases & Verticals

Eight verticals where hybrid IoT pays off, with the buyer pain and our angle for each.

Hybrid IoT Use Cases — Cheat Sheet

For: Sales reps. Match the customer's industry to the right pitch angle.

Asset tracking

The pain: Trailers, containers, plant equipment, ag machinery move in and out of cellular coverage every day. Cellular-only trackers stop reporting when assets sit at a remote yard, paddock or worksite.

The 9604 angle: Position + status messages keep flowing even when the asset is parked off-grid. Customer pays cellular rates 95% of the time, satellite rates only when satellite is the only link available.

Talking points: Cost-controlled fallback · global coverage on a single SKU · GNSS integrated · no separate satellite tracker needed.

Remote monitoring (utilities, water, energy)

The pain: Pump stations, tank-level monitors, reservoir telemetry, solar farms, remote substations. Often deployed in regional or sub-regional sites with patchy cellular. Customer either accepts outages, deploys a separate satellite radio, or hires drive-by data collection.

The 9604 angle: Single device. Cellular when reachable, satellite when not. Low-data SBD packets are perfect for telemetry payloads (sensor reads, alarms, watchdog pings).

Talking points: Reduced truck rolls · service-level guarantees · simpler BOM than dual-stack designs.

Agtech

The pain: Cattle trackers, paddock sensors, irrigation controllers, soil-moisture stations, weather monitors. Deployed on properties that may span tens of kilometres with zero cellular tower in sight.

The 9604 angle: One module, both radios, runs off solar + battery. Cellular at the homestead and along the road, satellite out in the back paddock. Position data for moving livestock.

Talking points: Energy-efficient · low-data plans match telemetry payloads · works across AU and NZ.

Field safety / lone worker

The pain: Workers in remote environments — mining, exploration, marine, emergency response, forestry. Cellular safety devices fail in dead zones. SOS, location and check-in messages must get through.

The 9604 angle: Cellular for cost-efficient regular check-ins. Iridium SBD for SOS and location when cellular drops. GNSS for accurate position.

Talking points: Duty of care · WHS compliance · satellite-grade reliability with cellular-grade economics.

Fleet and logistics (rural / regional)

The pain: Trucks running interstate or regional routes pass through cellular gaps. Driver hours, ELD, refrigeration sensors and load-status messages can go silent for hours.

The 9604 angle: Continuous status reporting across the entire route. Operations dashboards stop "going dark" between cities.

Talking points: Compliance · cold-chain integrity · driver safety · single airtime relationship.

OEMs, SIs, VARs and platform companies

The pain: Customers asking for a device that "just works everywhere." Building a separate cellular-and-satellite SKU is expensive. Maintaining two airtime contracts is operational drag.

The 9604 angle: Engineer once, ship one SKU. M2M provides both airtime layers under one commercial relationship. We can support the partner with hardware advice, dev kits and integration patterns.

Talking points: Embedded design simplicity · single-vendor commercial · channel margin on both layers.

Iridium 9604 — One-page Product Explainer

What it is

The Iridium 9604 is a hybrid IoT module that combines three radios in a single unit:

  • Cat-M1 / LTE-M — low-power cellular for everyday IoT

What it does

Lets a single IoT device run on cellular wherever cellular works, and automatically fall back to Iridium satellite when the device moves beyond coverage. Position-aware, low-power, low-data, global.

Who it suits

Cellular IoT (Cat-M1 / LTE-M / NB-IoT) covers the vast majority of urban and regional deployments at low cost. But the moment an asset crosses into a blackspot, a remote site, or a mobile route that wanders past coverage, cellular-only stops working.

05.2

Qualification Questions

A 12-question discovery script with a built-in scoring rule to qualify in 5–7 minutes.

Qualification Questions — Discovery Script

For: Sales reps. Use on initial discovery calls or in-person meetings.

The goal is to qualify in 5–7 minutes whether a Hybrid IoT Readiness Review is worth booking. Not every customer needs satellite — but the ones who do should self-identify quickly.

Block 1 — What are they connecting?

  1. "What kinds of devices are you connecting today?" (Asset tracker / sensor / vehicle / safety device / utility asset / industrial controller)

  2. "How many devices, roughly — hundreds, thousands, more?"

  3. "Are these existing devices, or are you planning a new product or upgrade?"

Block 2 — Where do they operate?

  1. "Where do those devices spend most of their time?" (Metro / regional / remote / mobile route / offshore / mixed)

  2. "Do any of them go beyond reliable cellular coverage?" (This is the qualifying question. A "yes" → keep pushing. A "no" → reposition cellular-only.)

  3. "When that happens, what's the current solution? Do you accept the outage, do you have a separate satellite radio, or do you collect data manually?"

Block 3 — What's the data profile?

  1. "What's the message size — alerts, telemetry, location pings, or richer payloads?"

  2. "How often does each device transmit? Every few minutes, hourly, daily, on-event?"

(Iridium SBD is best for small payloads, low frequency. If they're sending video or megabytes, hybrid SBD is the wrong fit — flag that and re-route to cellular-only.)

Block 4 — What's the cost of an outage?

  1. "When a device goes offline in a coverage gap, what's the operational impact?" (Lost data is fine / alert required / location required / operational issue / safety risk)

  2. "Does duty of care, compliance or SLA come into play?"

Block 5 — Decision shape

  1. "Are you the right person to talk to about hardware and connectivity, or is there a product owner / hardware engineer / operations lead we should loop in?"

  2. "What's your timeline — are you actively building something, evaluating, or planning ahead?"

Scoring the call (rep's own judgement)

Signal Weight
Devices already deployed in remote / regional / mobile environments High
Existing pain with coverage gaps that costs operational time or money High
Small payload size (alerts, telemetry, location) High
Active product build or upgrade cycle High
Customer pays for separate satellite radios today Medium-high
Vertical: fleet, utilities, agtech, safety, mining, marine Medium
OEM / SI / VAR — building for end-customer Medium
No existing coverage pain, no remote deployments Low → reposition

Decision

  • 3+ High signals → Book the Readiness Review.
  • 1–2 High signals → Send the use-case EDM, follow up in 2 weeks.
  • 0 High signals → Add to general nurture, focus on cellular-only opportunities.

Objection Handling

Seven canonical objections and the reframes that get the conversation back on track.

Objection Handling — Field Guide

For: Sales reps. The most common objections to hybrid IoT, and how to respond.

"Satellite is too expensive"

The objection: "We tried satellite years ago — the per-message cost made the project unviable."

The response: That was probably true. The 9604 is designed for hybrid economics: cellular is the default carrier and handles 95%+ of traffic. Satellite kicks in only when cellular fails. Customers typically see satellite spend in the tens of dollars per device per year, not hundreds.

Reframe: "You're not paying for satellite. You're paying for a cellular SLA you couldn't otherwise achieve."

"Our devices need more data than satellite can carry"

The objection: "We send images / video / large telemetry — SBD's tiny packet sizes won't work."

The response: Correct — Iridium SBD is for small messages (alerts, position, telemetry payloads). If the customer's payloads are megabytes, hybrid SBD is the wrong product. But — clarify what data actually needs to flow during a coverage gap. Often the rich payloads are non-critical; the alerts and locations are what matter. Use hybrid for the critical layer, cellular-only for the rich layer.

Reframe: "Most customers send full payloads on cellular and just-enough payloads on satellite. The 9604 lets you choose per-message."

"It sounds complex to build and maintain"

The objection: "Two radios, two airtime contracts, failover logic — too much complexity for a small engineering team."

The response: That's exactly the problem M2M solves. We provide: - One commercial relationship (cellular + satellite airtime under one M2M contract) - One device SKU (the 9604 ships with both radios) - Integration support — we share failover patterns and dev-kit examples

Reframe: "The hardware complexity went away when Iridium and Cat-M1 ended up in the same chip. The commercial complexity goes away when you run both layers through us."

"We already have a Cat-M1 / NB-IoT solution that works"

The objection: "Our devices use Cat-M1 / NB-IoT today and we're not seeing problems."

The response: Great — keep using it where it works. Hybrid isn't a rip-and-replace. Identify the 5–10% of deployments where cellular is unreliable, and trial the 9604 there. Once you've proven the model, you can converge on a single SKU for the next product generation.

Reframe: "Hybrid is your next-product story, not your this-product story. Let's review which deployments would benefit."

"Iridium is end-of-life / unstable"

The objection: "Aren't satellite networks consolidating? Will Iridium still be around in 10 years?"

The response: Iridium NEXT (66-satellite constellation, launched 2017–2019) is the most modern operational LEO IoT network. SBD is supported by Iridium's roadmap and underpins the 9604, the 9603 and a wide ecosystem of OEM modules. It's a multi-decade investment, not a sunset product.

Reframe: "Iridium NEXT is newer than most cellular networks the customer is already using."

"We're a small operator — does this even apply to us?"

The objection: "We're not a fleet of 50,000 devices. Is hybrid overkill?"

The response: Hybrid is more relevant for small operators. The big fleets can absorb coverage outages statistically. Small operators feel every dropped device — and every truck roll to retrieve one. A handful of 9604 devices in the field can be the difference between a usable product and an unreliable one.

Reframe: "You don't need to be big. You need to have devices that have to keep reporting."

"What about Swarm / Starlink / new LEO providers?"

The objection: "We're hearing about other satellite IoT options."

The response: They're real and we watch them. None match Iridium's combination of: - Global L-band coverage including polar regions - Real-time messaging latency (seconds, not minutes) - Established ecosystem of certified modules - Proven 25-year operational track record

For mission-critical alerts and position data, Iridium SBD is still the safe choice. We can talk through trade-offs at the Readiness Review.

"I need to think about it"

The reframe: Book the Readiness Review anyway. It's a 30-minute call, no commitment, and you'll know within that call whether hybrid is the right path or whether we should keep you on cellular-only. The cost of the call is much smaller than the cost of guessing wrong.

Data Quality Caveats

Field-coverage caveats and the seven things this scan can't tell you — read before reaching out.

Data quality caveats

What the data can't tell us yet — read before reaching out to anyone on the C-tier.

  1. AU instance classification fields are sparse. Industry 2.9%, Technologies 3.4%, Brand_s 4.8%, Customer Category 3.3%. The compound scoring rubric cannot reach A=70 for accounts whose classification fields are blank — they may still be perfect fits, just invisible to a structured scan. The NZ instance is dramatically better (Industry 86%, Product Requirements 99%).

  2. No deal / quote / order data available. AU Sales Orders + Quotes return zero records; NZ Sales Orders returns permission denied. We could not cross-reference which customers actually bought which products.

  3. Free-text scanning is keyword-naïve. A note that says "customer doesn't want satellite" still scored a satellite hit. Always sanity-check the snippet column before outreach.

  4. Inmarsat brand picklist never used. Across 10,695 AU instance accounts, zero have the Inmarsat brand tagged despite Inmarsat being in the picklist. Likely Inmarsat customers are hiding in free-text fields that this scan missed.

  5. Leads excluded by scope. 14,318 AU instance leads + 703 NZ instance leads were not scanned. Some recent leads showing hybrid interest may not appear on this list.

  6. 40,232 NZ SIM records not joined to accounts. Fast follow-up to identify NZ customers running specifically Cat-M1 / NB-IoT SIM rate plans today.

  7. No partner / vendor cross-reference. The dedicated Partners and Vendors modules were not extracted. Existing reseller relationships are not reflected in the Customer Category field.
