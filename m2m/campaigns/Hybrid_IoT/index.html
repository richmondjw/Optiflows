Week 1
Asset tracking
Trailers, containers, plant, ag equipment

The trailer does not stop where the tower stops.

Asset tracking works beautifully on cellular right up until the asset moves off the main freight route, into a yard on the outskirts of a regional town, or across a border overnight. Then the pings stop, the dashboard goes yellow, and someone spends a morning on the phone finding out where a $40,000 piece of plant actually is.

The fix is not to run satellite full-time. That is expensive, and cellular is doing the job 95% of the time. The fix is to run satellite as a low-cost backstop that wakes up when cellular does not.

● Cellular
$5.75–7.00
5MB–10MB Group
Position and status pings over cellular. Right-size to duty cycle — 5MB for hourly, 10MB for continuous.
● Satellite
$22
SBD-2.5 Pooled
Position packets when the asset sits off-grid. Size up to SBD-5 for assets in remote paddocks for extended periods.

Two networks, one dashboard, no gaps.

Week 2
Agtech
Paddock sensors, livestock, irrigation, weather

You can plan the property. You cannot plan the tower.

Every agtech deployment we see in Australia has the same shape: a strong cluster of sensors near the shed, another cluster near the pivot, and then a long tail of paddocks, tanks, and stock movements the cellular map didn't quite cover. The soil probe on the back block might be the most useful sensor on the farm, and it is also the one you can't reach.

Solar-powered, low-duty-cycle sensors do not need a fat pipe. They need a pipe that exists at all. Hybrid IoT gives them both.

● Cellular
$4.65–5.75
3MB–5MB Group
Solar-powered sensors on a low duty cycle. 3MB suits most sites; size to 5MB for livestock trackers or higher-frequency reporting.
● Satellite
$22
SBD-2.5 Pooled
Back-paddock telemetry beyond any tower. Size up to SBD-5 for livestock regularly reporting position off-grid.

Same sensor, same platform, one seamless read. Whether the tower is there or not.

Week 3
Remote monitoring
Pumps, tanks, substations, SCADA

The pump station is fine. Until it isn't.

By the time the SCADA screen shows red, the pump has been dry for four hours, the tank has overflowed into the paddock, or the substation alarm has been sitting quietly with nowhere to go.

Cellular carries 90% of SCADA telemetry across Australia today, and it does it well. The gap is not in the cities. The gap is at the sites that were built where the tower doesn't quite reach — the outer irrigation loop, the fire pump on the back of the property, the substation past the last relay.

● Cellular
$7.00–7.50
10MB–30MB Group
SCADA reads, periodic telemetry, event-driven alarms. Size down to 5MB for alarms-only sites.
● Satellite
$31
SBD-5 Pooled
Alarms and watchdog pings from low-coverage sites. Size up to SBD-10 for satellite-primary or high-alarm-frequency infrastructure.

The dashboard is the same. The alarm gets through either way.

Week 4
Field safety / lone worker
Mining, exploration, marine, forestry

Three of the four verticals in this series follow the same rule: run cellular as the primary network, keep satellite ready and sized modestly for the gaps. This one is the exception.

Field safety is the one vertical where we size satellite up. If the person carrying the device presses SOS, the packet has to get through. It cannot queue behind other traffic. It cannot fail because the plan was scaled for cost. It cannot depend on a tower being in range. And it definitely cannot be capacity-constrained on the moment it matters most.
● Cellular
$5.75
5MB Group
Regular, cost-efficient check-ins over cellular — position, status, and two-way messaging where coverage exists.
● Satellite
$39–46
SBD-10–15 Pooled
The one vertical where we size satellite UP. SOS and location must get through. You cannot be capacity-constrained on a safety packet. Do not size down.

If you deploy field-safety devices for mining crews, exploration teams, marine operators, or forestry, this is the pattern to build against.
