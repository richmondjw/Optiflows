The campaign in three lines. Everything else is execution.

Position the Iridium 9604 as the satellite half of every Australian IoT deployment where coverage or safety matters. Establish Hybrid IoT as a category — module + cellular + satellite + hardware advice + integration — with M2M as the single source.

The Proposition

One line. One offer. Six things we uniquely deliver.

The line

Cellular when you can. Satellite when you must.

One sentence. Same words everywhere — landing page, EDMs, sales calls, social posts.

What we're selling

The Iridium 9604 is a hybrid IoT module that puts three radios in a single unit:

  • Cat-M1 / LTE-M — low-power cellular for everyday IoT
  • Iridium SBD (Short Burst Data) — global satellite coverage for when cellular isn't available
  • GNSS — built-in location, no separate radio required

A device on the 9604 runs on cellular wherever cellular works and falls back to Iridium SBD automatically when it moves beyond coverage. Position-aware, low-power, low-data, global.

What makes the M2M story stronger than anyone else's

Recommended image: A shipping container or road-train trailer parked on the edge of a regional yard at dusk, dust track leading away, no visible mobile tower in shot. Alt option: yellow earthmoving plant on a mine access road with a wide sky.

The trailer does not stop where the tower stops.

Asset tracking works beautifully on cellular right up until the asset moves off the main freight route, into a yard on the outskirts of a regional town, or across a border overnight. Then the pings stop, the dashboard goes yellow, and someone spends a morning on the phone finding out where a $40,000 piece of plant actually is.

The fix is not to run satellite full-time. That is expensive, and cellular is doing the job 95% of the time. The fix is to run satellite as a low-cost backstop that wakes up when cellular does not.

Cellular first. Satellite ready. From about $28/device/month, combined.

You can plan the property. You cannot plan the tower.

The soil probe on the back block might be the most useful sensor on the farm, and it is also the one you can't reach.

Solar-powered, low-duty-cycle sensors do not need a fat pipe. They need a pipe that exists at all. Hybrid IoT gives them both — cellular first, satellite ready, from about $27/device/month combined.

Same sensor, same platform, one seamless read. Whether the tower is there or not.

Post platform: M2M One company page (Wednesday). James personal reshare Thursday.

Recommended image: A lonely pump station, water tank, or rural substation at golden hour, sky filling the top two-thirds of the frame, one small indicator light glowing on the equipment. No people. The pump station is fine. Until it isn't.

By the time the SCADA screen shows red, the pump has been dry for four hours, the tank has overflowed into the paddock, or the substation alarm has been sitting quietly with nowhere to go.

Cellular carries 90% of SCADA telemetry across Australia today, and it does it well. The gap is not in the cities. The gap is at the sites that were built where the tower doesn't quite reach — the outer irrigation loop, the fire pump on the back of the property, the substation past the last relay.

Hybrid IoT is the answer. Cellular for the routine reads, satellite as the alarm route. Combined bundles from about $38/device/month.

The dashboard is the same. The alarm gets through either way.

Week 4 · Field safety / lone worker

Post platform: M2M One company page (Tuesday). James personal reshare Wednesday.

Recommended image: A single high-vis figure walking a ridge, coastline, or forest track — silhouette or back-of-shot, no face visible. Wide landscape behind. Overlay in bottom-right: "Satellite you cannot size down." Muted, deliberate — this post carries the safety weight, so restrained composition matters more than colour. Alt option: a personal SOS device in the palm of a hand, worn gloves, everything else out of focus.

Three of the four verticals in our Hybrid IoT series follow the same rule: run cellular as the primary network, keep satellite ready and sized modestly for the gaps.

This one is the exception.

Field safety is the one vertical where we size satellite up.

If the person carrying the device presses SOS, the packet has to get through. It cannot queue behind other traffic. It cannot fail because the plan was scaled for cost. It cannot depend on a tower being in range. And it definitely cannot be capacity-constrained on the moment it matters most.

Cellular first, satellite ready. Combined safety-tier bundles from about $45/device/month — deliberately sized up on satellite. Do not size down.

If you deploy field-safety devices for mining crews, exploration teams, marine operators, or forestry, this is the pattern to build against.
