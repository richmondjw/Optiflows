Where does work get stuck in your business?

These are four versions of the same problem: the business has outgrown the way work moves through it.

01

Every approval comes back to one person

Quotes, discounts and sign-offs queue behind the owner or one manager. The team waits, then chases.

02

The same details get typed three times

An order lives in email, a spreadsheet and the accounts system. People copy it across, and the copies disagree.

03

Nobody knows where a job is up to

The only way to find out is to ask. Status lives in someone’s head or inbox, so handovers lose things.

04

You tried AI and the work didn’t change

A few people save time on their own tasks. The process around them is the same as it was.

How I work

Design the work first. Then automate it.

The method is design thinking applied to operations: understand how the work really happens, change one thing, test it, then build.

  1. Watch the work and map it

    I sit with the people doing the job and trace one process from the first request to the final decision. The map shows what happens now, including the workarounds.

  2. Redesign the handoff and test it

    We agree who owns each step and what they need to see. Then we try the change on a real handoff before anything is built.

  3. Automate what needs no judgement

    I connect the tools you already use so details are entered once. AI drafts, sorts and summarises. A named person still approves anything that goes to a customer or commits money.

Client work · 776BC, rowing apparel

One film in.
A campaign out.

776BC had a 22-second film and a familiar production problem: every reel, article and page built from it needed its own brief, and each one came back to the same people for review.

I redesigned it as one workflow. It takes the transcript and frames from the film, develops each channel version, then brings them to the 776BC team in a single shared review. One film became two reel edits, a long-read editorial, stills and a campaign page, and nobody briefed a piece from scratch.

Questions people ask first

What happens to our data?

I work inside the tools and accounts you already own. Before any of your information goes to an AI service, I tell you which service, what information and why, and you decide. Nothing is sent to a customer or changed in your records without a person approving it.

What does AI actually do in the work?

Routine reading and writing: drafting a reply, sorting requests, summarising a long thread, checking a form is complete. It doesn’t make decisions about money, customers or staff. Those stay with a named person.

What if the answer isn’t technology?

Then that’s the recommendation. Often the fix is agreeing who owns a step, or removing one. You don’t pay for a build you don’t need.

Which tools do you work with?

The ones you have. Most businesses this size run on email, spreadsheets, an accounts system and a CRM or job system. I connect those before suggesting anything new.

What does a build cost?

It depends on what the audit finds, so I quote it as a fixed scope afterwards, before you commit. The audit is priced so you can decide with the facts in front of you.

Do you work outside Melbourne?

Yes, with businesses anywhere in Australia. Interviews and readouts run well on video; I come on site in Melbourne when watching the work in person helps.
