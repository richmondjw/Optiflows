## 0. Blunt verdict — can the free pool sustain a premium brand?

**No. Not past ~6 weeks. And only if we're aggressive about how we use it.**

Here's the honest picture from two hours of mining Unsplash, Pexels, and Wikimedia Commons for Mornington Peninsula-specific imagery:

1. **The Peninsula is undercovered on stock platforms relative to its tourism profile.** Unsplash returns ~77 "Mornington Peninsula" photos total. Most of them are variations on the same three subjects: Cape Schanck Lighthouse, Sorrento cliffs, and back-beach sunsets. The Red Hill hinterland, cellar doors, dining scenes, and hotels are effectively absent. There is no hero image of a long lunch under vines. There is no Jackalope shot. There is no credible Peninsula Hot Springs image. There is no wine-country autumnal golden-hour landscape that would sell a cover story.

2. **Cape Schanck Lighthouse dominates the pool and is already over-rotated.** Nine of the ten cleanest editorial shots on Unsplash are variations of the same lighthouse from three vantage points. If we use them across Homepage + Do + Journal we're going to look like a Wikipedia article in three weeks. The lighthouse is a *supporting* image in our pack, not a flagship.

3. **Wikimedia Commons has the breadth (beaches, national park, Arthurs Seat, blowhole, heritage villages) but the taste is uneven.** Most of the Commons Peninsula imagery is panoramio-era amateur work — fine for encyclopaedias, wrong for a premium editorial brand. The good ones are real (Merricks Beach panorama, Pulpit Rock, Arthurs Seat above the clouds, Cape Schanck aerial 2024), and we should use them hard for 4 weeks while we make the shift.

4. **The venue layer is a dead zone.** Zero usable free images of Tedesca, Ten Minutes by Tractor, Pt Leo Estate, Montalto, Polperro, Lindenderry, Jackalope, Continental Sorrento, or any of the hat-ed restaurants. This is the layer a premium brand lives and dies on. We have to source these directly from venues' media kits or commission them.

5. **Rye/Red Hill/Merricks hinterland — the beating heart of the brand story — barely exists in the free pool.** A single "Mornington Peninsula vineyard.jpg" on Commons and one strawberry-farm banner. We're trying to tell a wine-country story without wine-country images. That's a branding problem, not a sourcing problem.

### Practical implication

**Use the free pack for the Phase 0 bridge and the first ~3–4 weeks of Phase 1. No longer.** Beyond that:

- **Pull from venue press/media kits** — every cellar door and most hotels have professional sets they'll give you for editorial coverage in exchange for a credit. This is the highest-leverage move we haven't yet made and is free.
- **Reach out to Visit Victoria / Tourism Mornington Peninsula's media library.** They license images free to editorial outlets with Peninsula-related coverage. Much stronger pool than public stock.
- **Revisit the Phase 1 paid photography decision inside 4 weeks.** The roadmap-locked "no paid shoot" decision was made under a specific cost-defer assumption. If the brand promise is going to hold, the decision is deferred, not deleted. Commissioned photography for the top 20 venues is still the single highest-leverage spend on the entire roadmap.

The free pool is a stabilizer, not a foundation.

## 1. Shortlist rules used (abbreviated)

Kept in: atmospheric, place-legible, editorial colour grade achievable, human scale without tourist clutter, compositional strength, seasonally resonant (autumn bias).

Cut on sight: drone postcard spam, overprocessed sunsets, generic "vineyard rows" that could be anywhere, wedding-style lifestyle shots, luxury-resort wallpaper, anything where the subject is a sign/monument/plaque.

Fit score: 1 = reject / 5 = launch-worthy right now.

Source shorthand:
- **U** = Unsplash (license: free, commercial use OK, no attribution required)
- **WC** = Wikimedia Commons (license varies — noted per image; most are CC-BY-SA or CC0)
- **PX** = Pexels (license: free, commercial use OK)

For every image I note whether it's **Peninsula-specific** (✅) or a **temporary bridge** (⚠️ / 🔁) — meaning it's good enough to use for 2–4 weeks but not a brand-defining image we'd be proud of at month 6.

## 8. Recommended next actions (executable this week)

1. **Adopt Pulpit Rock at Cape Schanck (J1/H2) as the Phase 0 homepage hero.** It is the single strongest free image with real place legibility.
2. **Retire the current Unsplash hero on the live site and push the Pulpit Rock image behind a credit line** ("Image: Wikimedia Commons / [photographer], CC BY-SA 4.0"). Build a simple `/credits` page the first time we do this — it becomes a trust surface.
3. **Email 8 venue marketing inboxes this week** (Jackalope, Lindenderry, Pt Leo, Montalto, Ten Minutes by Tractor, Polperro, Peninsula Hot Springs, Continental Sorrento) offering editorial coverage in exchange for media kit access. This is a 45-minute task that unblocks the single biggest imagery gap the brand has.
4. **Open a dialogue with Visit Mornington Peninsula and Visit Victoria tourism marketing teams** about accessing their press image library. This is standard practice for editorial travel outlets; they have better imagery than anything on Unsplash.
5. **Set a 4-week imagery review date** — 7 May 2026. At that review, if venue media kits have arrived and cover ≥60% of flagship pages, we can defer paid commissioned photography further. If they haven't, **revisit the roadmap's "no paid shoot" decision** against the then-current brand trajectory.
6. **Put a 2-week lifespan on every bridge image.** Tag them in an image credits log with an expiry date. Nothing in this pack should still be live on 1 June 2026 without a deliberate review.
