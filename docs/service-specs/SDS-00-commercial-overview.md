## Revenue Architecture

The three offers are designed to work as a pipeline, not as independent products.

```
Audit ($5,500)
  → converts to →
System Install ($25,000)
  → converts to →
Operating Partner ($6,500–$9,500/mo × 12–24 months)
```

**Lifetime client value (conservative):**
- Audit: $5,500
- Install: $25,000
- Operating Partner (18 months at Tier 2): $117,000

**Lifetime client value (optimistic — 2 installs + 24mo OP Tier 3):**
- Audit: $6,500
- Install ×2: $60,000
- Operating Partner (24 months at Tier 3): $228,000
- **Total LTV: ~$294,500**

## Pricing Logic

### The Audit is a qualifier, not a profit centre
At $5,500, the Audit makes a small margin on James's time. The real value is that it:
1. Qualifies the client (are they buildable?)
2. Generates the brief for the Install (we know exactly what to build)
3. Creates trust before the bigger commercial conversation

Do not discount the Audit. Do not offer it free. Clients who pay for the Audit are serious. Clients who want it free rarely convert.

### The Install is the primary revenue event
The Install is where OptiFlows makes money and proves value. Price it based on complexity, not on hours. The floor is $18k, the ceiling is $32k. Most installs should land at $24–28k.

Never negotiate price without negotiating scope. If a client pushes back on price, offer a narrower scope — not a discount.

### The Operating Partner is the compounding bet
At $6,500/month, a single Operating Partner client running for 24 months is worth $156k. The margin is strong (40–50% blended), the delivery rhythm is predictable, and the IP generated is proprietary.

Target: 4–6 Operating Partner clients as the steady-state business. Everything else (Audits, Installs) is the pipeline that feeds it.

## Bespoke Engagement

Not every client problem fits the standard ladder. Bespoke engagements are designed from scratch for:
- Multi-team or multi-business-unit complexity
- Parallel workflow builds that require coordinated architecture
- Businesses with internal technical teams who need advisory or design review, not a build partner
- Operating model redesigns that go beyond one or two workflows
- Unusual timelines, constraints, or scopes that don't fit fixed packages

Bespoke engagements are scoped and priced after an initial conversation. No templates. The deliverables, structure, and timeline are agreed before anything is signed.

## Pricing Policy

**Pricing is not published publicly.** It is shared in conversation, after a discovery call or Audit, when we understand the scope. This protects against:
- Clients self-selecting out before a conversation that might reveal the value
- Price anchoring before we understand the problem
- Racing to the bottom on the wrong metric

Internal pricing guidance is in each SDS document. What goes to clients is a scoped proposal with a fixed number and a defined scope — not a rate card.

## Commercial Principles

**1. Fixed scope, fixed price — always.**
No time-and-materials. No "we'll see how it goes." Every engagement has a defined scope, a defined price, and a defined output. Clients know what they're getting. James knows what he's delivering.

**2. Never discount without reducing scope.**
Price is a signal. Discounting signals that the price was wrong. If a client can't afford the price, offer a smaller scope — not the same scope for less.

**3. The Audit is not optional for System Installs where we don't know the workflow.**
Installing a system without a proper diagnostic is how projects fail. If a client wants to skip the Audit, charge a premium for the compressed discovery week built into the Install.

**4. Upfront payment on Audits. 50/50 on Installs.**
Never do an Audit on credit. The 50% deposit on Installs covers James's time cost through Week 3. The second 50% is due on delivery of the system (not sign-off — delivery).

**5. Operating Partner is by application.**
Not every Install client becomes an Operating Partner. The conversation is had at the Install handoff. If they're not right for it, don't push it. A bad Operating Partner relationship is worse than no relationship.

## What We Don't Do

These are scope boundaries. Knowing what's out of scope is as important as knowing what's in.

| Out of scope | Reason | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Running systems for clients (long-term ops) | Creates dependency, not capability | Operating Partner builds their capability |
| Sourcing or purchasing their SaaS tools | Not our expertise; creates procurement complexity | Advise, don't procure |
| Staff recruitment or HR | Wrong domain | Refer to HR/recruitment firms |
| Data migration | High risk, low leverage | Bring in a specialist |
| Generic AI strategy without a workflow focus | Too vague to deliver | Reframe toward specific friction points |
| Pilot projects with no path to production | We don't do pilots | Only engage on production installs |

## The OptiFlows Guarantee

Every System Install includes this guarantee:

> "If the system we build doesn't meet the success criteria we agreed in Week 2, we'll fix it. No charge. We stay until it works."

This is not a commercial risk — it's a positioning statement. If the success criteria are defined clearly and the client has done their part (internal owner, access, availability), the system will work. The guarantee is a commitment to quality, not a commercial exposure.

## Referral & Partnership Policy

- **Client referrals:** Any OptiFlows client who refers a new System Install client receives a 5% fee credit on their next invoice ($1,250 on a $25k Install). Payable in service credit, not cash.
- **Referral partners (accountants, business advisors, consultants):** 7% of the first Install fee as a cash referral fee. Not applicable to Operating Partner revenue.
- **Technology partners:** No formal program yet. Build relationships first, formalise later.
