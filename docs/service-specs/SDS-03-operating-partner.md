## 2. Purpose & Positioning

The Operating Partner is where the business model compounds. The Audit qualifies, the Install proves value, the Operating Partner builds the moat.

This is not a support retainer. It's not a "call us if something breaks" arrangement. It is an embedded operating function — James (and over time, a structured OptiFlows team) functions as the client's head of AI operations.

The Operating Partner exists because the hardest part of AI implementation isn't the first system. It's the second, third, and fourth — and the organisational capability to keep building. Without an Operating Partner, clients plateau after one Install. With it, they compound.

**This offer is by application.** Not every System Install client is right for it. The Operating Partner works when:
- Leadership is genuinely committed to AI as a strategic capability (not just cost-cutting)
- The business is growing fast enough to have a pipeline of workflow problems worth fixing
- The internal owner from the Install has demonstrated they can hold the system
- There is executive sponsorship (MD or equivalent is the sponsor, not just an ops person)

## 3. Client Profile

**Ideal Operating Partner client:**
- Has completed a System Install and it's running well
- Has 2–4 more workflows they want to fix in the next 12 months
- Wants to build internal capability, not permanent dependency on OptiFlows
- Has a champion internally who is excited about this, not just tolerating it
- Revenue: $5M–$100M AUD, growing
- Decision-maker is an active sponsor, not just a budget approver

**Not a fit:**
- Post-Install, system isn't running (fix that first)
- No internal owner (Operating Partner without one is a crutch, not a build)
- MD sees AI as a cost-reduction exercise only (misaligned on outcomes)
- Business is contracting or in distress (wrong time)

## 4. Engagement Structure

### Monthly Operating Rhythm

Every month, regardless of tier, includes:

**Week 1: Operations review**
- Review system performance: what ran, what broke, what was slow
- Review the audit log: any anomalies, governance issues, human interventions required
- Identify optimisation opportunities in existing systems

**Week 2: Build / expand work** (Tier 2 and 3 only in active quarters)
- Active build work on the next workflow installation
- Follows SDS-02 process, compressed into the monthly retainer rhythm

**Week 3: Async / delivery week**
- James working in the background: building, testing, refining
- Internal owner runs existing systems
- Async comms only (daily 3-sentence update if active build)

**Week 4: Monthly review + planning**
- 60-min monthly review call (James + MD + internal owner)
- Review: what was delivered, what's running, what metrics moved
- Plan: what's next for the following month
- Decide: any scope changes, tier adjustments, or new priorities

## 5. Tier Detail

### Tier 1 — Maintain ($4,500/mo)
**For:** Post-Install clients who want systems kept healthy and evolving slowly

**What's included:**
- System monitoring: James reviews audit logs weekly, flags issues
- Minor updates: prompt tuning, routing adjustments, minor integration changes (up to 4h/mo)
- Monthly review call (60 min)
- Async support (response within 24h business hours)
- Quarterly optimisation session (2h — deeper review, identify improvement opportunities)

**What's not included:**
- New system builds
- Major architectural changes
- Staff training for new team members (charged separately at $300/h)

**James time:** ~8–10h/month
**Margin at $4,500:** ~55–65% (strong)

---

### Tier 2 — Expand ($6,500/mo)
**For:** Clients actively building out their operating system — one new workflow per quarter

**What's included:**
- Everything in Tier 1
- One new System Install per quarter, delivered within the retainer (equivalent to a ~$25k build at no additional cost — the value is in the compounding)
- Design sessions for upcoming builds (so Weeks 3–5 of each quarter have build momentum)
- Access to OptiFlows' growing library of integration patterns and agent prompts

**Build rhythm:**
- Month 1 of quarter: design and scope new system
- Month 2: build and integrate
- Month 3: test, train, handoff — new system is running by end of quarter

**James time:** ~20–28h/month (spikes in build months)
**Margin at $6,500 (build month):** ~30–40%
**Margin at $6,500 (non-build month):** ~65%
**Blended quarterly margin:** ~48%

---

### Tier 3 — Build ($9,500/mo)
**For:** Businesses moving fast — want to build internal AI capability, not just buy installed systems

**What's included:**
- Everything in Tier 2 (one new system per quarter)
- Capability uplift sessions (2h/month): training the internal owner to design and run diagnostics themselves — the goal is to make OptiFlows progressively less necessary for diagnosis and design, while James focuses on complex builds
- Ad-hoc build support (up to 8 additional hours/month)
- Priority scheduling (Tier 3 clients get first pick on James's calendar)
- Access to OptiFlows' playbook library in full
- Annual operating strategy review (3h, quarterly check-in at strategic level)

**What this tier is building toward:**
By month 12, a well-run Tier 3 engagement should result in the client having:
- 4+ working AI-native systems in production
- An internal owner who can design new workflows and run diagnostics without OptiFlows
- A documented operating model they own
- James transitioning to advisory/complex-build-only (fewer hours, higher leverage)

**James time:** ~28–36h/month
**Margin at $9,500:** ~32–52% depending on month

## 11. Relationship Management

The Operating Partner is a relationship product. The commercial relationship is the easy part.

**What makes it work:**
- James must be genuinely invested in the client's operational success — not just delivering hours
- Monthly review calls must be strategic, not status updates
- When something breaks (and it will), own it fast and fix it fast
- When the client is ready to go it alone, say so — don't hold on for the revenue

**What kills it:**
- Becoming a support desk (scope this out hard)
- Losing the internal owner (if they leave, the engagement is at risk — flag immediately)
- MD disengages (project becomes orphaned — have the conversation early)
- No new systems being built (Tier 2/3 clients will disengage if nothing is progressing)

---

## 12. Graduation & Offboarding

The goal of every Operating Partner engagement is to make the client progressively less dependent on James for routine work, while James's contribution becomes higher-leverage over time.

**Graduation criteria:**
- Internal owner can run diagnostics, design new workflows, and manage existing systems without James
- 4+ systems in production and running stably
- Client has documented their own operating model
- James's value is now at the architectural/strategic layer, not the build layer

**Graduation options:**
1. **Stay at reduced tier** — Tier 1 (maintain) is the steady-state for mature clients
2. **Advisory only** — James available for complex builds or major new initiatives, not on retainer
3. **Full independence** — Client runs everything themselves; door is open if they want to come back

Graduation is a success, not a loss. Document it. Use it in sales.
