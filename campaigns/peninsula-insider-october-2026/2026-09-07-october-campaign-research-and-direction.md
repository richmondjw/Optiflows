# October 2026 campaign research and recommended direction

## Decision summary

Proceed to commissioning with a four-part October programme called **October, in long form**.

The public-facing line is **One more hour. Use it well.**

The campaign should move Peninsula Insider from September's constructed still-life system into honest, editorial photography. It should show the real texture of the Peninsula in October: longer evening light, Western Port's quieter cultural life, Mornington community activity, and a coast experienced over distance. It must not use a generated image to impersonate a real place, event, venue or person.

This is not a generic spring campaign. It is a month about what becomes possible when the day stretches out and the Peninsula's calendar deepens.

### Recommended four-part programme

| Release window | Chapter | Reader decision | Primary evidence | Content action | Release state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 4 October | **One more hour** | Where will I use the first longer evening? | Daylight saving begins 4 October; school holidays end 4 October | Refresh and consolidate the existing spring, late-afternoon walk and sunset coverage | Ready for commissioning, subject to route and venue reverification |
| 5 to 11 October | **Read the other coast** | Which Western Port Writes session and day suit me? | Western Port Writes, 9 to 11 October | Create one practical festival decision guide and a compact Somers plan | Hold pending Coolart access confirmation |
| 12 to 18 October | **Keep the weekend** | Do I join the community walk, take the garden Sunday, or combine both? | Breast Foot Forward, 17 October; Rose Garden High Tea, 18 October; Main Street festival cancellation | Build a current Mornington weekend guide and route high-intent cancellation traffic into it | Ready for commissioning, with booking checks |
| 19 to 25 October | **The long finish** | Am I participating, supporting, or following the coast? | Bloody Long Walk, 25 October; H&H at Stonier, 22 to 25 October | Create a useful event and route guide; treat H&H as an optional cultural layer only if access is confirmed | Bloody Long Walk confirmed; H&H availability hold |

An optional 31 October Halloweekend Story can sit outside the four-part spine. It should not become a fifth campaign chapter unless the primary programme changes.

## Why this direction is stronger

1. **It belongs to October.** Daylight saving starts on Sunday 4 October 2026, the same day the school-holiday period ends. The shift from holiday mode to longer local evenings gives the month a clear behavioural opening.
2. **It advances September instead of repeating it.** September's programme already covered walks, school-holiday planning and a general spring reset. October should be more editorial, more place-specific and more culturally grounded.
3. **It gives photography a job.** Real photography is not decoration here. Each chapter depends on a distinct place, time of day and human-scale choice.
4. **It is useful before it is promotional.** Every chapter resolves a practical question about timing, access, booking, distance or what to combine.
5. **It can survive event change.** The month has one strong non-event opening and clear fallback paths for the two uncertain cultural events.

## Evidence-led opportunity assessment

### 1. One more hour

**Classification:** confirmed opportunity.

Victoria advances clocks by one hour at 2:00 am AEST on Sunday 4 October 2026. Victorian government school Term 4 starts Monday 5 October. That creates a clean audience transition: the family holiday period ends, but usable evening light expands.

The PI content library already contains:

- `the-spring-peninsula.md`
- `the-peninsulas-best-late-afternoon-walks.md`
- `the-sunset-hour.md`
- `the-sunset-drink.md`

In the inspected local source snapshot, all four were last verified on 22 April 2026. Three also carry temporary or unresolved image licensing labels. October should refresh and consolidate these pages rather than create another broad spring article.

**Editorial proposition:** not another list of sunsets. Give readers three after-five plans with real duration, access, parking, safety, food and weather alternatives.

**Photography proposition:** late light as lived experience. Photograph the spare jumper on a car seat, coastal scrub in cross-light, a real track junction, water on a jetty edge, and the last occupied table outside. Avoid the generic orange-sky postcard.

### 2. Read the other coast

**Classification:** strongly supported opportunity with a material access contradiction.

Western Port Writes states that its opening event is at Somers General on Friday 9 October and its festival programme is at Coolart on Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October. Tickets and day passes are listed, and the programme warns that Coolart has no food venue, with lunch boxes, coffee and picnic planning provided separately.

However, Parks Victoria currently lists Coolart Historic Area as closed until further notice because of protected industrial action. Its page also carries a narrower Homestead opening notice, creating an internally awkward access picture. The event organiser's programme and the land manager's closure notice cannot both be treated as sufficient proof that ordinary festival access is settled.

**Release gate:** obtain a current public organiser notice or direct written confirmation that the festival is proceeding at the advertised venue. Recheck Parks Victoria immediately before publication. No PI copy should imply Coolart access until that gate passes.

**Fallback if unresolved by 22 September:** retain the chapter title but publish a Western Port reading and picnic field guide centred on publicly accessible Somers and Balnarring locations. Do not use Coolart photography or access claims.

**Photography proposition:** real pages, hands and coastal detail paired with Western Port's flatter light and quieter horizon. Event-day crowd photography requires organiser permission and releases where needed. Pre-event location photography must not be presented as festival reportage.

### 3. Keep the weekend

**Classification:** confirmed opportunity with an honest cancellation context.

The 2026 Main Street Mornington Festival is cancelled. PI's existing cancellation page is already a high-intent discovery surface and was the strongest page in the latest available 90-day SEO report, with 19 clicks and 561 impressions. The correct move is not to minimise the cancellation. It is to acknowledge it once, then help the reader make a useful weekend.

Two current anchors exist:

- Breast Foot Forward runs Saturday 17 October from 8:30 am to noon, with 5 km and 10 km starts and a Rosebud Village Green finish. The organiser states that event profit supports locals undergoing breast cancer treatment.
- Mornington Botanical Rose Gardens lists its monthly High Tea for Sunday 18 October.

**Editorial proposition:** a practical, positive Mornington weekend with purpose. Make it clear that these are separate events, with separate bookings and access arrangements. Do not call either a replacement for the cancelled festival.

**Photography proposition:** community without staging a fake event. Use commissioned or organiser-cleared event imagery, real Mornington street detail, the rose garden in current flowering condition, hands tying walking shoes and a simple table study. Any recognisable participant needs an appropriate release or organiser-supplied permission.

### 4. The long finish

**Classification:** confirmed primary opportunity with one optional held layer.

The Bloody Long Walk is scheduled for Sunday 25 October. Its official page describes a 35 km supported route from Quarantine Station through Sorrento, Rye, Rosebud, Dromana and Safety Beach to Martha Cove Marina. It provides a useful Peninsula-wide narrative and a clear decision for participants, supporters and readers following the route.

H&H Festivals also lists a Stonier programme from Thursday 22 to Sunday 25 October. Its official contact page is currently framed around joining a waiting list. PI should not build a primary call to action around tickets that may not be available.

**Editorial proposition:** lead with the walk. Explain registration, transport, start and finish, support options and what non-participants need to know. Add H&H only as an editorial note for existing ticket holders or if saleable inventory is confirmed before lock.

**Photography proposition:** document distance through a sequence of real coastal frames rather than one heroic landscape. Photograph the start context, track or foreshore texture, midpoint fatigue cues, wayfinding and the finish environment. A pre-event route shoot must be labelled as route photography, not as images of the 2026 walk.

## Photography-first visual system

### Creative territory: Long Frames

Use a restrained editorial system built from actual Peninsula photography:

- one wide establishing photograph for each chapter;
- one human-scale action frame;
- two close details taken in the same place or journey;
- a contact-sheet or folio treatment that identifies the chapter, date and practical choice;
- generous negative space and the current PI wordmark;
- natural October colour, including overcast and wind, rather than forcing permanent golden weather;
- copy, rules, crop marks and typography applied deterministically after photography is approved.

The folio treatment must not invent camera data, timestamps or documentary circumstances. It is graphic navigation, not fake evidence.

This gives the campaign a recognisable visual language while allowing every image to remain truthful. Abstract frames are encouraged when they come from the actual place: tea-tree shadow, water movement, folded paper, weatherboard, table linen, path gravel, rose detail, vineyard wire or a wet foreshore. An abstract image must not carry a location claim it cannot prove.

### What not to do

- No AI-generated or AI-reconstructed view of a named Peninsula place.
- No generated crowd, event, business interior, food item or recognisable person presented as real.
- No scraped image, screenshot, social post or search result used as a production master.
- No heavy sky replacement, synthetic weather, added wildlife or fabricated seasonal colour.
- No use of a licensed source image as input to an image-generation or model-training service unless its licence explicitly permits that use.
- No generic luxury-tourism collage. The visual value should come from observation and sequence.

### Motion and video

Capture six to ten seconds of real motion alongside every hero setup: water, grass, pages, walking feet, fabric, steam or changing light. Use simple cuts, restrained pans, typographic reveals and crop movement. Do not synthetically animate a still photograph of a real location. This produces honest Reels and Stories without needing generative video.

## Image acquisition and rights ladder

Use sources in this order:

1. **PI-owned photography:** James, Emma or an assigned PI photographer, with creator, capture date, place and usage permission recorded.
2. **Commissioned local photography:** a bounded shot list and written assignment covering web, organic social, newsletter, crop, colour correction and archive use. Spend and contracting require approval.
3. **Event organiser media:** only with written permission or a clear press licence. Preserve required credits, usage limits and expiry.
4. **Visit Victoria Content Hub:** use only after account approval and an item-level terms check. The platform's terms impose work-specific restrictions and prohibit machine-learning use. Do not pass these assets through Higgsfield or another generative service.
5. **Explicit Creative Commons sources:** verify the exact item, author, licence version, derivatives permission and attribution.
6. **Search and scraping:** reference-board use only. It can inform a shot list but cannot establish publication rights.

Every selected image needs a ledger entry containing source URL or asset ID, creator, capture date if known, depicted location, licence, permitted channels, required credit, model/property release status, edits allowed, expiry, reviewer and evidence file.

## Proposed photo commission

### Shoot A: One more hour

- Port Phillip-side evening, with one current walk and one town edge.
- Wide coastal light, real wayfinding, clothing/weather detail and a usable after-five food stop exterior.
- Capture both clear and overcast treatments if conditions permit.

### Shoot B: Read the other coast

- Somers and Western Port details, plus Coolart only if public access is confirmed.
- Book, paper, hands, picnic preparation, coastal vegetation and the flatter Western Port horizon.
- No pre-event frame may imply that the festival was in progress.

### Shoot C: Keep the weekend

- Current Mornington streetscape and Rose Garden condition.
- Community-action detail from Breast Foot Forward only through organiser-cleared access, or use a clearly pre-event preparation frame.
- Avoid identifiable passers-by without releases.

### Shoot D: The long finish

- Four real route contexts from Point Nepean to Martha Cove.
- Movement, footwear, wayfinding, water, distance and arrival.
- Observe park restrictions and do not stage subjects in unsafe or restricted areas.

Each shoot should supply native horizontal, vertical and square-safe frames. Do not rely on one landscape crop to serve every channel.

## Measurement plan

Set targets only after the September campaign baseline is available. October should measure:

- homepage and Insider Picks click-through to the campaign hub;
- campaign-hub click-through to each guide;
- article-to-event and article-to-related-PI-page clicks;
- saves and My Trip actions where instrumentation exists;
- newsletter click-through by chapter;
- Instagram saves, shares and completion through the carousel;
- Facebook link clicks;
- event status corrections made before publication;
- percentage of published campaign imagery with a complete rights ledger;
- return visits within 7 and 28 days.

Use tagged owned-channel links and one measurement dictionary. Do not treat social reach alone as campaign success.
